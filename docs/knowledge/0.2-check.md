history closed. Write answers on paper or in a scratch file, then grade
against `0.2-answers.md`.

or make a degraded array worse.

## A. Teach-back (15)

1. (6) On a blank page, draw the storage stack of this host from physical
   disks to mount points, for both tiers. Include array names, RAID
   levels, volume groups, every logical volume and where each is mounted.
2. (6) List the build steps of this module in order, one line each, with
   the reason for each step and the reason for the order.
3. (3) On a scratch volume group or by writing the commands only: create a
   thin pool, create a thin volume in it, snapshot the volume, roll back,
   remove everything.

## B. Concept questions (20, two points each)

1. What is the RAID5 write hole? State the exact sequence of events
   needed for it to corrupt data.
2. What does a write-intent bitmap do, and why does it not close the
   write hole?
3. Why can PPL and a bitmap not be enabled on the same array, and what do
   you give up by choosing PPL?
4. Why is `root` a thick volume outside the thin pool?
5. Why is about 180 GiB of `ssd-vg` left unallocated?
6. A thin pool can run out in two different ways. Name both and say what
   happens to the VMs.
7. Why were the Seagate disks' kernel timeouts raised to 180 seconds?
   What exactly goes wrong with the default?
8. What is the difference between `qemu:///system` and `qemu:///session`,
   and how did that difference show up during the build?
9. `/srv/bulk/repo` was created only after `/srv/bulk` was mounted. What
   would have happened if the copy had been done before the mount?
10. Why does the EFI partition's array use metadata 1.0 when everything
    else uses 1.2?

## C. Read this command (10, two points each)

For each: explain every token, and say what it prints or changes.

1. `echo check | sudo tee /sys/block/md124/md/sync_action`
2. `sudo lvcreate --type thin --thinpool vmpool -V 40G -n vm-a ssd-vg`
3. `sudo mdadm --detail --scan | grep hdd | sudo tee -a /etc/mdadm.conf`
4. `ACTION=="add|change", SUBSYSTEM=="block", KERNEL=="sd[a-z]", ATTRS{model}=="ST1000DM010*", ATTR{device/timeout}="180"`
5. `sudo smartctl -l selftest -d sat /dev/disk/by-id/ata-Samsung_SSD_850_PRO_1TB_S3D2NX0J603337T`

## D. Pick the tool (10, two points each)

No commands are given. Name the tool and the form of command you would
run first.

1. A new NIC was installed. You need to know whether the kernel sees it
   and which driver it is using.
2. You have a disk in your hand with a label that reads only a serial
   number. You need to know which arrays it belongs to on the running
   host.
3. `/etc/fstab` needs a new entry for a volume. You need the value that
   goes in the first field.
4. A colleague says a service "is enabled but isn't running after the
   reboot". What two commands tell you what is true, and where do you look
   for the reason?
5. A disk's device name keeps changing and a rule you wrote for it never
   seems to apply. How do you find out what attributes you can match on,
   and how do you see what your rule actually does?

2. (2) The three Seagate disks are replaced with NAS disks that support

## F. Failure scenarios (10, two points each)

For each: what happened, what the blast radius is, and what you do first.

1. After a power cut the host comes back. `cat /proc/mdstat` shows `pv00`
   resyncing. An hour later one SSD reports unreadable sectors.
2. `sudo lvs ssd-vg` shows `vmpool` at `Data% 99.80`. VMs have stopped
   responding. The host itself is fine.
3. `/proc/mdstat` shows `md124 : active raid5 sdd[0] sde[1]` with
   `[3/2] [UU_]`.
4. After a reboot the console shows `Cannot open access to console, the
   root account is locked` and the host never reaches a login prompt.
5. `sudo dnf install tmux` fails with `Curl error (37): Couldn't read a
   file:// file`.
